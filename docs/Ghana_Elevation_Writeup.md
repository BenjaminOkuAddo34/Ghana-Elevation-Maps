# Ghana’s Elevation in Context

A short write-up to accompany the Ghana elevation maps and 3D relief model.

## At a glance

| | Ghana | Africa |
|---|---|---|
| Mean elevation | about 190 m | roughly 600 m (estimates range from about 580 to 700 m) |
| Highest point | about 880 to 885 m, traditionally Mount Afadja (disputed, see below) | Mount Kilimanjaro, 5,895 m (Tanzania) |
| Lowest point | 0 m, the Gulf of Guinea coast | Lake Assal, about 155 m below sea level (Djibouti) |

## A low country in a high continent

Africa is a high continent, with a mean elevation of roughly 600 m, similar to North and South America and well below Asia’s 950 m. Geographers often split it into “High Africa” in the south and east, where plateaus average around 1,000 m, and “Low Africa” in the north and west. Ghana sits firmly in Low Africa.

Ghana’s mean elevation of about 190 m is only about a third of the continental average. Even its highest summit, at around 885 m on the traditional figure, is lower than the mean of Africa’s high plateaus and roughly one sixth the height of Kilimanjaro. Half of the country lies below about 152 m. The reason is geological: the ancient Precambrian rock beneath most of Ghana has been worn down by erosion almost to a plain.

A scale model makes the point. Shrink Ghana to a model 715 mm tall, matching its 715 km length, and the highest peak would stand less than 1 mm above the sea.

## Where Ghana is lowest

The lowest ground is the Atlantic coast along the Gulf of Guinea, at sea level. Low plains stretch across much of the south, and the coastal plains from Accra east toward the Volta delta and Keta are flat and close to sea level. The road from Accra to Keta even detours inland to avoid the lowest-lying ground.

## Where Ghana is highest

The high ground lies mainly along the eastern edge and in the south-central interior:

- **The Akwapim-Togo Ranges** run along the border with Togo in the Volta Region. They hold Mount Afadja, traditionally Ghana’s highest mountain, and nearby Mount Aduadu at about 746 m.
- **The Kwahu Plateau** is a dissected plateau in the south-central part of the country.
- **The Gambaga Escarpment** rises in the north-east.

## Which peak is really the highest?

This is less settled than it looks. The traditional figure for Mount Afadja (also written Afadjato) is 880 to 885 m, and the CIA World Factbook still lists 885 m. Newer satellite-based elevation models place the Afadja summit much lower, at roughly 557 to 587 m, and the current Wikipedia entry for the mountain gives 587 m. Those same sources point to Leklata, a peak on the Togo border about 4.6 km from Afadja, as Ghana’s highest, reported at roughly 900 m in one source.

The practical lesson is that high-point figures depend on the dataset and on how the summit was measured. Global elevation models such as the one used in this project are surface models at coarse resolution, so treat national extremes as approximate.

## What the maps show

The elevation and topographic maps use Copernicus GLO-30 data, resampled to 90 m. They show a mostly low, gently undulating country, with the main relief concentrated in a band along the Togo border and the Kwahu Plateau. Because 880 m of relief is spread across 715 km, the 3D model uses strong vertical exaggeration (about 27 times at the current setting) so the topography is visible at all.
